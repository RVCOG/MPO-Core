# MPO Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) Reference Guide
The *MPO CFR Reference Guide* is a comprehensive regulatory reference and compliance management resource that catalogs the federal regulations most relevant to Metropolitan Planning Organizations (MPOs) and Transportation Management Areas (TMAs). The guide is organized by CFR citation and provides a structured summary of each regulation's purpose, applicability, compliance requirements, affected planning documents, review cycles, responsible agencies, and direct links to the Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (eCFR). The inventory is centered on the metropolitan transportation planning framework established in 23 CFR Part 450 and incorporates related requirements from Titles 23, 40, and 49, including transportation conformity, performance management, transit asset management, transit safety, and Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS).
 

The guide is intended to: 
- **Act as a Central Hub** 
  Provide a single, organized source for MPO-related federal regulations, guidance, and compliance requirements across Titles 23, 40, and 49 of the Code of Federal Regulations. By consolidating regulatory references into one location, the guide reduces the need to search across multiple federal websites and regulatory documents, improving consistency and accessibility for users.
- **Support MPO Staff During Daily Tasks**
  Serve as a practical reference tool for planners, program managers, GIS staff, public involvement specialists, executive leadership, consultants, and compliance personnel. The guide helps staff quickly identify applicable regulations, understand planning requirements, determine responsible agencies, verify update cycles, and locate authoritative regulatory sources when performing transportation planning, programming, reporting, and certification-related activities.
- **Provide a Public Resource that Compiles MPO Regulations**
  Make MPO-related federal regulations more transparent and understandable to elected officials, partner agencies, stakeholders, consultants, and the general public. The guide presents complex regulatory requirements in a structured and user-friendly format, helping users understand how federal regulations influence transportation planning and decision-making.
- **Improve Regulatory Awareness and Understanding**
  Help users understand the purpose, applicability, and intent of key MPO regulations, including planning, programming, performance management, transportation conformity, transit asset management, transit safety, and congestion management requirements.
- **Serve as a Training and Onboarding Resource**
  Provide a foundational resource for new employees, board members, committee members, and consultants who need to become familiar with MPO planning requirements and federal transportation regulations.
- **Support Planning Document Development**
  Help staff identify requirements associated with key planning products, including the Unified Planning Work Program (UPWP), Metropolitan Transportation Plan (MTP), Transportation Improvement Program (TIP), Public Participation Plan (PPP), Congestion Management Process (CMP), conformity determinations, and performance target reporting.
- **Provide Direct Access to Authoritative Sources**
  Include direct links to the Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (eCFR) for each citation, enabling users to quickly access the official regulatory language and verify requirements using the most current federal rulemaking information.


The *MPO CFR Reference Guide* serves as a centralized compliance matrix and regulatory roadmap for transportation planning practitioners, enabling efficient identification of federal planning requirements, documentation obligations, coordination responsibilities, and certification expectations across the metropolitan transportation planning process. It is designed to improve regulatory consistency, reduce compliance risk, and support transparent, performance-based transportation decision-making.

## CFR Reference Table
### How to Read CFR Reference Table
- **Lead Agency** = Who owns compliance?
- **Applicability** = Who must comply?
- **Mandatory/Optional** = Is the action required?
- **Planning Document Impacted** = Where must evidence appear?
- **Review Cycle** = When must compliance be demonstrated?
